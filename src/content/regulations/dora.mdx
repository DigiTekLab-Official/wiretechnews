## Executive Summary & Enterprise Risk Profile

The Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) shifts the regulatory focus of the EU financial sector from financial capital resilience to pure IT operational resilience. DORA mandates that financial entities—and critically, their critical ICT third-party service providers (CTPPs)—maintain robust architectures capable of withstanding, responding to, and recovering from severe cyber threats.

The operational impact extends far beyond banks, directly regulating cloud service providers, data analytics firms, and software vendors operating within the financial supply chain.

## Core Pillars of Operational Compliance

Enterprise IT and security leadership must align their operations with DORA's five central pillars:

### 1. ICT Risk Management
Financial entities must establish a comprehensive governance framework overseen by the management body.
* **Mandates:** Continuous identification of critical assets, implementation of resilient network architectures, and development of rapid recovery protocols.
* **Impact:** Requires a unified IT asset register and the deprecation of legacy systems that cannot support continuous monitoring.

### 2. Incident Reporting Protocols
DORA harmonizes cyber incident reporting across the EU, replacing fragmented national directives.
* **Mandates:** Organizations must classify ICT-related incidents and report major events to competent authorities within strictly defined, rapid timelines.
* **Impact:** Requires automated SIEM/SOAR integration and unified incident response playbooks to prevent reporting delays.

### 3. Digital Operational Resilience Testing
Routine vulnerability scanning is no longer legally sufficient for critical infrastructure.
* **Mandates:** Execution of advanced, Threat-Led Penetration Testing (TLPT) at least every three years for critical entities.
* **Impact:** Requires engaging certified external red-teams and coordinating live-environment tests alongside primary ICT vendors without disrupting live financial services.

### 4. ICT Third-Party Risk Management
This is DORA's most disruptive enterprise mandate. Financial entities are held accountable for the resilience of their supply chains.
* **Mandates:** Strict contractual clauses governing access, recovery, and audit rights. Maintenance of an updated Register of Information encompassing all third-party dependencies.
* **Impact:** Mandates the renegotiation of existing SaaS and cloud SLAs to guarantee compliance with DORA audit and exit strategies.

### 5. Information Sharing
Encourages the establishment of structured, intelligence-sharing arrangements among financial entities to preemptively neutralize persistent cyber threats.

## Vendor Reclassification: The CTPP Framework

Cloud hyperscalers and critical software vendors designated as "Critical ICT Third-Party Providers" (CTPPs) will face direct regulatory oversight by European Supervisory Authorities (ESAs). This bypasses the traditional model of regulating vendors solely through their enterprise clients, creating a direct compliance liability for international enterprise tech organizations.
