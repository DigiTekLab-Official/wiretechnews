## Overview

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP Act) establishes India's primary framework governing digital personal data processing, consent management, individual rights, and organisational accountability.

Operationalised through the DPDP Rules 2025, the framework creates legal obligations for organisations processing digital personal data in connection with offering goods or services within India.

The law applies to entities defined as *Data Fiduciaries* and introduces governance obligations involving:
- consent management
- breach notification
- security safeguards
- data minimisation
- rights handling
- accountability controls

## Territorial Scope

The DPDP framework applies to:

- Organisations processing digital personal data within India
- Entities offering goods or services to individuals in India
- Cross-border digital service providers handling Indian user data
- Platforms processing digital personal data connected to Indian operations

The framework therefore impacts:
- SaaS companies
- fintech platforms
- ecommerce businesses
- AI service providers
- cloud infrastructure vendors
- ad-tech platforms
- digital marketplaces

## Key Principles

The DPDP framework is built around seven foundational principles:

1. Consent and transparency
2. Purpose limitation
3. Data minimisation
4. Accuracy
5. Storage limitation
6. Reasonable security safeguards
7. Accountability

These principles govern the full lifecycle of digital personal data processing.

## Consent Requirements

Data Fiduciaries must provide clear consent notices explaining:

- what personal data is collected
- the purpose of processing
- how data will be used
- how consent may be withdrawn

Consent mechanisms must remain:
- specific
- informed
- unambiguous
- accessible

The framework also formally recognises *Consent Managers* that help individuals manage and withdraw consent across platforms.

## Data Principal Rights

The framework grants individuals several rights involving their personal data.

These include:

- Right to access information
- Right to correction and updating
- Right to erasure in certain circumstances
- Right to grievance redressal
- Right to nominate another person to exercise rights

Organisations must establish operational mechanisms for handling these requests.

## Breach Notification

Where a personal data breach occurs, affected individuals and the Data Protection Board of India may require notification without undue delay.

Incident response processes should therefore include:

- breach assessment workflows
- legal escalation procedures
- customer communication templates
- impact evaluation processes
- audit logging

## Significant Data Fiduciaries

The framework imposes enhanced compliance obligations on designated Significant Data Fiduciaries.

Additional obligations may include:

- periodic audits
- impact assessments
- enhanced governance controls
- stricter compliance oversight
- additional security obligations

Large-scale digital platforms and high-volume processors are likely to receive heightened regulatory scrutiny.

## Enforcement

The framework establishes the *Data Protection Board of India* as the primary enforcement authority.

The Board may:
- investigate complaints
- examine breaches
- impose penalties
- issue compliance directions
- oversee grievance handling

The operational model is designed as a largely digital-first regulatory system.

## Penalties

The DPDP framework includes substantial financial penalties.

Maximum penalties may reach:

- ₹250 crore for failure to implement reasonable security safeguards
- ₹200 crore for breach notification failures and certain compliance violations

The framework primarily relies on financial and operational enforcement mechanisms rather than criminal liability.
