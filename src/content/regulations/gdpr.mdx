  - question: Who does the GDPR apply to?
    answer: The GDPR applies to any organisation established in the EU/EEA, and to organisations outside the EU/EEA that offer goods or services to, or monitor the behaviour of, individuals in the EU/EEA — regardless of where the organisation is located.
  - question: What is the maximum GDPR fine?
    answer: The highest tier of fines is up to €20 million or 4% of the undertaking's total worldwide annual turnover in the preceding financial year, whichever is higher. A lower tier (€10M or 2%) applies to specific violations such as controller/processor obligations and certification matters.
  - question: What is a lawful basis for processing?
    answer: There are six lawful bases under Article 6 — consent, contract performance, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, and legitimate interests. Organisations must identify a lawful basis before processing personal data and document their reasoning. Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous.
  - question: When must a data breach be reported?
    answer: Controllers must notify their supervisory authority of a personal data breach within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, where feasible. If the breach is likely to result in a high risk to individuals, affected data subjects must also be notified without undue delay.
  - question: What is a Data Protection Officer (DPO)?
    answer: A DPO is a designated individual responsible for overseeing GDPR compliance. Appointment is mandatory for public authorities, organisations that process personal data on a large scale as a core activity, or organisations that process special categories of data systematically on a large scale.

## Overview

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is the European Union's primary data protection law, establishing a unified legal framework for personal data processing across EU and EEA member states. It applies extraterritorially to any organisation processing personal data of individuals in the EU, regardless of where the organisation is based.

The GDPR came into force on 25 May 2018, replacing the 1995 Data Protection Directive. It introduced significantly stricter requirements, substantially higher penalties, mandatory accountability mechanisms, and new individual rights compared to its predecessor.

## Territorial Scope

The GDPR applies to:

- **Establishments in the EU/EEA** — any controller or processor with an establishment in the EU/EEA, regardless of where processing takes place
- **Offering goods or services** — organisations outside the EU that offer goods or services (whether paid or free) to individuals in the EU
- **Monitoring behaviour** — organisations outside the EU that monitor the behaviour of individuals in the EU (including tracking cookies, profiling, analytics)

## Key Principles

The GDPR is built on seven core principles (Article 5):

1. **Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency** — processing must have a legal basis and be transparent to data subjects
2. **Purpose limitation** — data may only be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes
3. **Data minimisation** — only the minimum data necessary for the stated purpose may be collected
4. **Accuracy** — personal data must be accurate and kept up to date
5. **Storage limitation** — data must not be retained longer than necessary
6. **Integrity and confidentiality** — appropriate security measures must be implemented
7. **Accountability** — controllers must be able to demonstrate compliance

## Individual Rights

The GDPR grants data subjects eight key rights:
- Right of access (Art. 15)
- Right to rectification (Art. 16)
- Right to erasure / "right to be forgotten" (Art. 17)
- Right to restriction of processing (Art. 18)
- Right to data portability (Art. 20)
- Right to object (Art. 21)
- Rights related to automated decision-making (Art. 22)
- Right to withdraw consent

## International Data Transfers

Transfers of personal data to third countries (outside the EU/EEA) require one of the following safeguards:
- **Adequacy decision** — European Commission has determined the destination country offers adequate protection
- **Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)** — EU-approved contract terms
- **Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs)** — approved intra-group transfer mechanism
- **Certification/codes of conduct** — approved under GDPR mechanisms

The 2022 EU-US Data Privacy Framework restored a key adequacy mechanism for US transfers following the invalidation of Privacy Shield in *Schrems II* (2020).
