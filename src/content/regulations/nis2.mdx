## Executive Summary & Enterprise Risk Profile

The Network and Information Security Directive 2 (NIS2) fundamentally expands the scope of European cybersecurity regulation. By replacing the original NIS Directive, it drastically increases the number of regulated entities, bringing mid-sized and large enterprises across diverse sectors (e.g., manufacturing, digital providers, food supply) under strict cybersecurity governance.

Crucially, NIS2 introduces **direct personal liability** for corporate management regarding cybersecurity negligence, fundamentally shifting cyber risk from an IT operational issue to a Board-level legal liability.

## Entity Classification: Essential vs. Important

NIS2 abandons the previous "operator of essential services" terminology, utilizing a size-cap rule to automatically categorize medium and large enterprises into two risk tiers:

### 1. Essential Entities
Subject to the highest level of proactive supervision and stringent enforcement.
* **Sectors:** Energy, Transport, Banking/Financial Markets (where DORA does not preempt), Health, Digital Infrastructure (Cloud providers, DNS service providers), and Public Administration.
* **Supervision:** Proactive, routine audits and continuous regulatory oversight.

### 2. Important Entities
Subject to reactive supervision, meaning regulators will primarily investigate post-incident.
* **Sectors:** Postal services, Waste management, Chemicals, Food production, Manufacturing of critical products, and Digital providers (Search engines, online marketplaces).

## Core Operational Mandates

To achieve compliance, enterprise architectures must enforce the following security controls:

1. **Supply Chain Security:** Enterprises must assess the systemic vulnerabilities introduced by their direct suppliers and service providers. This requires continuous third-party risk monitoring (TPRM).
2. **Aggressive Incident Reporting:** * An "early warning" must be submitted to the CSIRT within **24 hours** of becoming aware of a significant incident.
   * A full incident notification must follow within **72 hours**.
   * A final comprehensive report is required within **1 month**.
3. **Basic Cyber Hygiene Practices:** Mandatory implementation of Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), continuous vulnerability handling, basic computer training, and secured communications.
4. **Management Accountability:** The management bodies (C-Suite/Board) must approve cybersecurity risk-management measures, oversee their implementation, and undergo mandatory cybersecurity training. Failure to comply can result in temporary bans on executives holding management positions.

## Fines and Enforcement

Non-compliance carries severe financial and operational penalties:
* **Essential Entities:** Fines up to €10 million or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.
* **Important Entities:** Fines up to €7 million or 1.4% of total worldwide annual turnover.
