## Enterprise Definition

A Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is a mandatory enterprise risk management process designed to identify, analyze, and mitigate the privacy risks associated with data processing activities. Under Article 35 of the EU and UK GDPR, a DPIA is not merely a compliance checklist; it is a legally required operational framework that must be integrated directly into the software development lifecycle (SDLC) and procurement pipelines before any high-risk processing begins.

## Mandatory Trigger Conditions

Enterprise IT and data governance teams must initiate a DPIA when processing is "likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons." Regulators explicitly mandate DPIAs in the following scenarios:

* **Systematic Profiling:** Automated processing or profiling that produces legal or similarly significant effects (e.g., AI-driven credit scoring, automated recruitment filtering).
* **Large-Scale Processing of Special Categories:** Processing massive volumes of sensitive data (e.g., biometric databases, healthcare telemetry, political affiliations).
* **Systematic Public Monitoring:** Large-scale surveillance of publicly accessible areas (e.g., municipal CCTV networks, smart-city facial recognition).

> **Operational Note:** If a DPIA identifies high residual risks that the enterprise cannot adequately mitigate through architectural or policy controls, the Data Controller must consult the relevant Supervisory Authority (e.g., the ICO in the UK) prior to deployment. 

## Architectural Components of a DPIA

A compliant assessment must legally contain, at minimum:
1.  A systematic description of the processing operations and their strategic purposes.
2.  An assessment of the necessity and proportionality of the operations.
3.  An assessment of the risks to the rights and freedoms of data subjects.
4.  The specific cryptographic, architectural, and policy measures envisaged to mitigate the identified risks.
